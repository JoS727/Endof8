OB’s Storm Preparation Needs to Reach Beyond One Saturday

Local news and commentary by Joseph Calitoy
November 16, 2026

If you missed a sandbag pickup on Saturday, you still need to know how to protect your home on Monday.

San Diego announced that Robb Athletic Field would be one of five locations distributing free, pre-filled sandbags on November 14, with a limit of 10 per household while supplies lasted. The City says it plans to continue Saturday distributions at those locations through the end of the year, with additional dates and details to be announced. The announcement does not establish how many households received bags or whether supplies are available now. City announcement

The useful question now is what happens between those Saturdays.

For someone working during a distribution window, without transportation, or unable to carry the bags, a date and location may not be enough. Those are access questions worth answering before residents are trying to solve them in heavy rain.

The City’s broader preparations include coastal sand berms. In September, it described work along beaches and the Mission Bay shoreline intended to reduce damage from high tides and surf, with completion anticipated by early November. That was a published schedule, not a neighborhood-by-neighborhood completion report. I have not independently verified the condition of the berms today. City shoreline-preparation report

There are also limits residents deserve to hear plainly. The City says its public stormwater system is designed for typical rain events and can be overwhelmed by excessive rainfall, especially when it arrives quickly. Preparation can reduce risk without guaranteeing that every street or property will stay dry. City preparedness guidance

For OB and Point Loma, I want the next round of public information to be useful at the level of an actual household: where supplies are available now, how to check before making the trip, and what assistance exists for someone who cannot collect them.

The information should also make clear which problems belong in a service request and which require an emergency call.

San Diego directs residents to Get It Done for non-emergency concerns such as blocked storm drains, fallen trees and potholes. Immediate threats to public safety belong with 911. Its guidance also advises residents to keep at least three days of emergency supplies, secure outdoor items, clear gutters and drains on their own property, and know their evacuation routes. During storms, residents should avoid floodwater and stay away from storm channels. City reporting and safety guidance

Those are manageable steps when people have time and clear information.

I would like the City to keep a dated, easy-to-find neighborhood update throughout the season, including changes to supply locations and known access problems. A resident should not have to sort through expired event announcements to work out where help is available.

Residents can also check on a neighbor before conditions worsen. Ask whether they have the information and assistance they need. Do that from safety, without entering floodwater or attempting dangerous repairs.

The people who organized the sandbag distribution deserve credit for making preparation more accessible. Keeping that help accessible after the event deserves the same attention.

For current resources, start with the City’s storm-preparedness page, rather than assuming a previous pickup is still operating.
